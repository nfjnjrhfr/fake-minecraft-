Hollywood

5 seasons · sealed bids
You run a studio. Buy the talent, read the audience, release one film a season.
How it works

Everything about a film is bought before anyone knows what audiences will want. The talent is auctioned in sealed bids — you never see what the other studios offered until the lot is gone.

  • Five seasons. Each season three lots go up: scripts, stars and directors, each with a genre and a quality of 1–10.
  • You bid in millions. High bid takes the lot and pays what it bid. Ties go to the studio that bid first, which is never you.
  • Then you cut one film from what you own: a script is required, a star and a director are optional and are consumed with it.
  • Gross = the talent on screen × how hot that genre is this season × luck. Genre heat moves every season; the tracking report guesses next season's, badly.
  • You are scored on profit — gross minus production minus what you paid at auction. A masterpiece bought at a stupid price is still a loss.
