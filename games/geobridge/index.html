How it works

Every town is worth its population, but only if it can reach the capital over routes you have built. What you are really buying is a connected graph, and the cheapest connected graph is a tree — which is also the one a flood can cut in half.

  • Routes are priced by length, by the climb they cross, and worst of all by the river — a bridge costs roughly two and a half times a road.
  • The price on the map is an estimate. The real bill lands when you build, anywhere from 35% under to 35% over.
  • A survey costs 8% of the estimate and pins the real number before you commit.
  • When you open the network the spring flood takes out one bridge, chosen at random. Towns cut off from the capital score nothing.
  • Score = population still connected + a tenth of the money you never spent.

A second bridge you never needed is insurance. Insurance you didn't need is wasted budget. That is the whole game.
