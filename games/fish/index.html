How it works

Nobody owns the water. Every fleet's catch comes out of the same stock, and what is left regrows before the next season — fastest when the stock sits near the middle of its range, barely at all once it is stripped.

  • Regrowth is logistic: S ← S + 0.6·S·(1 − S/10000), so total landings above roughly 1,500 t a season eat into the stock.
  • If the fleets ask for more fish than exist, the catch is shared out pro-rata and the stock craters.
  • Below 500 t the fishery collapses — it closes for good and nobody lands anything else.
  • Whatever is still swimming at the end is sold off as quota rights worth 25% of the final stock — but the split is weighted by who actually kept to the announced caps. Break every cap and you get nothing from it.
  • An announced cap is not binding. The other skippers weigh your record of keeping your own word.

You are measured against 4,500 t — a quarter of everything a perfectly managed fishery could have yielded in twelve seasons. Fishing flat out in a fishery that collapses lands roughly 3,200 t, and beats the other three fleets while doing it. Placing first is not the same as doing well.
